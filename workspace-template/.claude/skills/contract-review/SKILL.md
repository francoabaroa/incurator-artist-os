# Contract Review

Expert analysis of music industry agreements with systematic red flag identification and negotiation guidance.

**CRITICAL DISCLAIMER**: This guidance is for informational purposes only and does NOT constitute legal advice. Always consult with a qualified entertainment attorney before signing any contract.

## When to Use This Skill

- Artist provides contract text, clauses, or term sheets
- Questions about deal types (360, licensing, distribution, management, publishing)
- Need to understand rights (masters, publishing, sync, mechanical, performance)
- Evaluating whether terms are "standard" or predatory
- Preparing questions for an entertainment lawyer

## Triage: First 60 Seconds

Before diving into details, establish context:

1. **What type of deal is this?**
   - Recording (label) → Focus on masters, advances, recoupment, options
   - Publishing → Focus on admin share, term, territory, reversion
   - Distribution → Focus on term, exclusivity, revenue split, rights
   - Management → Focus on commission %, sunset clause, scope
   - Sync/Licensing → Focus on term, exclusivity, usage rights, territory
   - 360 → Focus on ALL revenue streams and what label actually provides

2. **What stage is the artist?**
   - Unsigned/indie → More leverage to walk away, should protect upside
   - Emerging (some traction) → Negotiate hard, this deal sets precedent
   - Established → Different risk profile, focus on creative control

3. **What's the urgency?**
   - "They need an answer by Friday" → RED FLAG. Good deals don't expire in days.
   - No rush → Take time. Always say "I need to have my lawyer review this."

## The Decision Framework

### Step 1: Identify the Exchange

Every contract is an exchange. Clarify:
- **What is the artist GIVING?** (Rights, revenue %, exclusivity, creative control)
- **What is the artist GETTING?** (Money, services, access, support)
- **Is this exchange FAIR?** (Compare to industry standards below)

### Step 2: Find the "Forever" Terms

Scan for these words/phrases that indicate permanent or near-permanent commitments:
- "Life of copyright" / "perpetuity" → You lose control FOREVER
- "In perpetuity throughout the universe" → Maximum rights grab
- "Irrevocable" → Cannot be undone
- "Work for hire" → You never owned it in the first place

**If you see these → Major negotiation point or walk away.**

### Step 3: Check the Money Math

Ask these questions in order:
1. **What's the advance?** (Upfront money)
2. **What's recoupable?** (What gets paid back from your royalties)
3. **What's the royalty rate?** (Your cut after recoupment)
4. **What costs are charged to you?** (Marketing, videos, tour support)

**The trap**: Large advance + high recoupable costs + low royalty rate = You never see another check.

### Step 4: Map the Options

"Options" are the label's right to sign you for MORE albums:
- **Who has the option?** (If only the label, you're locked in; they're not)
- **How many options?** (1-2 is reasonable; 5+ is indentured servitude)
- **What triggers the option?** (Sales threshold? Or just their whim?)

## The Big 5 Red Flags (Ranked by Severity)

### 1. 🚨 Perpetuity / Life of Copyright (CRITICAL)
- **What it means**: They own your recordings forever. Your grandchildren won't get them back.
- **Why it's devastating**: You can't re-record, re-release, or benefit from catalog value.
- **The fix**: Demand a "reversion clause" — rights return after 7-15 years OR upon recoupment.
- **Negotiation script**: "I understand you need to recoup your investment. Can we tie the term to recoupment plus 3-5 years, rather than life of copyright?"

### 2. 🚨 Cross-Collateralization (HIGH)
- **What it means**: Debt from Album A is paid off by revenue from Album B.
- **Why it's devastating**: One flop means you never recoup. Even if Album B is a hit, it pays for Album A's losses.
- **The fix**: Request "un-crossed" or per-project accounting.
- **Negotiation script**: "I'd like each project to stand on its own. Can we account for each album separately?"

### 3. 🚨 360 Grab Without Services (HIGH)
- **What it means**: Label takes 10-25% of touring, merch, publishing, sponsorships.
- **Why it's problematic**: They're taking revenue from areas where they may provide ZERO support.
- **The fix**: 360 only makes sense if they're actively building those revenue streams. Limit scope to areas where they provide direct investment.
- **Negotiation script**: "I'm open to a 360 structure, but only on revenue streams where you're actively providing services. Can we make touring/merch participation contingent on you providing support in those areas?"

### 4. ⚠️ Controlled Composition Clause (MEDIUM)
- **What it means**: You get paid only 75% (or less) of statutory mechanical rate for songs you wrote.
- **Why it's problematic**: You're being penalized for being a songwriter-artist.
- **The fix**: Strike the clause entirely. Fight for full statutory rate.
- **Context**: This is becoming less common in the streaming era but still appears.

### 5. ⚠️ Audit Restrictions (MEDIUM)
- **What it means**: Limited or no right to audit the label's books.
- **Why it's problematic**: You can't verify if you're being paid correctly.
- **The fix**: Ensure you have the right to audit at least once per year, with reasonable notice.
- **Negotiation script**: "Standard practice is annual audit rights. Can we include that?"

## Deal Type Cheat Sheets

### Recording Deal (Label)
| Term | Artist-Friendly | Industry Standard | Red Flag |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|----------|
| Royalty Rate | 20%+ | 15-18% | <12% |
| Term | 1 album firm | 1+2 options | 1+5 options |
| Rights Reversion | Upon recoupment | 7-10 years post-release | Life of copyright |
| Advance Recoupment | Advance only | Advance + some costs | All costs |
| Creative Control | Artist approval | Mutual approval | Label approval |

### Distribution Deal
| Term | Artist-Friendly | Industry Standard | Red Flag |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|----------|
| Revenue Split | 90/10 artist | 80/20 artist | <70% artist |
| Term | 1 year | 2-3 years | 5+ years |
| Exclusivity | Non-exclusive | Semi-exclusive | Full catalog exclusive |
| Rights | Distribution only | Distribution + marketing | Any master ownership |

### Management Deal
| Term | Artist-Friendly | Industry Standard | Red Flag |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|----------|
| Commission | 10-15% | 15-20% | >20% |
| Commission Base | Net after costs | Gross income | Gross + publishing |
| Sunset Clause | Yes (declining %) | Partial | None (they earn forever) |
| Term | 1-2 years | 2-3 years | 5+ years |

### Publishing Deal
| Term | Artist-Friendly | Industry Standard | Red Flag |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|----------|
| Split | 80/20 (you/pub) | 75/25 | <70% writer share |
| Term | 2-3 years | 3-5 years | 10+ years |
| Reversion | Post-term | 5-10 years post-term | Life of copyright |
| Advance | Yes | Yes | No advance + bad split |

## Anti-Patterns: What NOT to Do

❌ **Don't sign under pressure.** "This offer expires Friday" is a manipulation tactic.
❌ **Don't assume "standard" means "fair."** Standard for the label ≠ standard for the artist.
❌ **Don't skip the lawyer.** The $500-1500 for a contract review can save you millions.
❌ **Don't focus only on the advance.** The advance is the distraction; the terms are the trap.
❌ **Don't sign exclusivity without leverage.** If they want exclusive, what are they giving?
❌ **Don't believe verbal promises.** "We'll work something out later" = It won't happen.

## Workflow

1. **Intake & Triage**
   - What type of deal? What's the artist's career stage? What's the urgency?
   - Ask the artist to paste the full contract OR specific clauses they're confused about.

2. **Systematic Analysis**
   - Use the Decision Framework (Exchange → Forever Terms → Money Math → Options)
   - Check against the Big 5 Red Flags
   - Compare to Deal Type Cheat Sheets

3. **Deliverables**
   - **Summary**: 2-3 sentence plain-English explanation of the deal
   - **Flags**: List of concerning clauses with severity ratings
   - **Questions for Lawyer**: Specific questions the artist should ask their attorney
   - **Negotiation Scripts**: If applicable, suggested language to push back

4. **Update Workspace**
   - Create tasks in `tasks/backlog.json` (e.g., "Schedule lawyer consultation", "Draft counter-proposal on reversion clause")
   - Do NOT persist raw contract text in the workspace for privacy

## Output Files

- `tasks/backlog.json` - Negotiation and legal follow-up tasks
