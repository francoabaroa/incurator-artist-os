# Contract Red Flags: Deep Dive

This reference provides clause-by-clause analysis for common contract red flags.

## 1. Perpetuity / Life of Copyright Clauses

### What to Look For

**Exact phrases that signal perpetuity:**
- "for the life of the copyright"
- "in perpetuity"
- "forever" / "for all time"
- "throughout the universe in perpetuity"
- "irrevocably assign"
- "work made for hire" (you never owned it)

**Hidden perpetuity:**
- No explicit term stated (defaults to perpetuity in many jurisdictions)
- "Until [impossible condition]" (e.g., "until all copies are sold")
- "Automatically renewing" without termination right

### Why This Matters (The Math)

Copyright for sound recordings lasts 70-95+ years. If you sign perpetuity at age 25:
- You lose control for 70+ years
- Your estate/heirs inherit nothing
- Catalog value compounds (old music becomes more valuable)
- You can't benefit from future exploitation methods (think: streaming wasn't foreseen in 2000)

### Negotiation Tactics

**Tier 1 (Best):** Rights revert upon full recoupment
**Tier 2 (Good):** Rights revert after X years post-release (7-15 typical)
**Tier 3 (Acceptable):** Rights revert after term + sunset period
**Unacceptable:** Life of copyright with no reversion

**Script:**
> "I understand the need to recoup investment. I'd propose a reversion clause where rights return to me after recoupment plus a reasonable holdback period—say, 5 years. This protects your investment while ensuring I eventually regain my work."

## 2. Cross-Collateralization

### What to Look For

**Explicit cross-collat language:**
- "All advances under this Agreement shall be cross-collateralized"
- "Recoupable against all royalties payable under this and any other agreement"
- "Accounting shall be on an aggregate basis"
- "Combined accounting" / "consolidated accounting"

**Hidden cross-collat:**
- Multiple albums under one "master agreement"
- Options that reference "continuation" of existing debt
- References to "prior agreements" in recoupment language

### The Devastating Example

Without cross-collateralization:
| Album | Advance | Earnings | Artist Gets |
|-------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Album A | $100K | $50K | $0 (still recouping) |
| Album B | $100K | $300K | $100K profit |

With cross-collateralization:
| Album | Advance | Earnings | Artist Gets |
|-------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Album A | $100K | $50K | $0 |
| Album B | $100K | $300K | $50K (paying off A's debt) |

**Net loss to artist: $50,000**

### Negotiation Tactics

**Script:**
> "I'd like each project to be accounted for separately. My concern is that one underperforming release shouldn't impact the success of another. Can we structure this with per-project recoupment?"

## 3. 360 Deal Provisions

### What to Look For

**Revenue streams they're taking:**
- Recordings (standard)
- Live performance / touring (360 grab)
- Merchandising (360 grab)
- Publishing / songwriting (360 grab—very aggressive)
- Sponsorships / endorsements (360 grab)
- Fan club / direct-to-fan (360 grab)
- Acting / modeling (extreme grab)

**The key question:** Are they ACTIVELY supporting each stream they're taking?

### 360 Deal Evaluation Matrix

| Revenue Stream | They Provide Support? | Fair Commission |
|----------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Recordings | Yes (always) | 15-20% royalty |
| Touring | Book shows? Advance tour support? | 10-15% IF yes |
| Merch | Design? Manufacturing? Distribution? | 15-20% IF yes |
| Publishing | Active sync pitching? Admin? | Usually separate deal |
| Sponsorships | Actively securing deals? | 10-15% IF yes |

### Negotiation Tactics

**Script:**
> "I'm open to a 360 structure, but I'd like to tie your participation in each stream to active support. For touring, if you're providing tour support or booking assistance, a commission makes sense. Otherwise, I'd prefer to carve that out. Can we make each stream contingent on your active involvement?"

## 4. Options and Commitment Periods

### What to Look For

**One-sided options:**
- "Label shall have the exclusive option to require Artist to record..."
- Only the label can exercise the option (artist has no choice)
- No performance threshold required to exercise

**Excessive options:**
- More than 2-3 options on top of the initial commitment
- Each option = another album you're obligated to deliver
- 1+5 options = potentially 6 albums you can't escape

**Option exercise terms:**
- Short exercise window (30 days) = pressure tactics
- No notification requirement = uncertainty

### The Hidden Trap

"1 album + 4 options" sounds like "maybe 5 albums." It actually means:
- You MUST deliver up to 5 albums if they want them
- They can DROP you at any point
- You have no power to leave for a better deal
- If you become successful, you're locked into terms negotiated when you had no leverage

### Negotiation Tactics

**Script:**
> "I'm comfortable with a 1+2 structure, but I'd like to add a performance threshold for option exercise—for example, the option can only be exercised if the prior album achieves X streams or Y sales within the first 18 months. This aligns our incentives."

## 5. Controlled Composition Clause

### What to Look For

**Exact language:**
- "Controlled compositions shall be paid at 75% of the statutory rate"
- "Mechanical royalties for controlled compositions shall not exceed..."
- "Cap of 10 compositions per album" (regardless of actual songs)

### Why This Matters

- Statutory mechanical rate (2024): ~$0.12 per song per copy
- Controlled comp at 75%: ~$0.09 per song per copy
- On a 12-song album you wrote: You lose $0.36 per album sold
- On 100,000 units: You lose $36,000

### Negotiation Tactics

**Script:**
> "I'd like to receive full statutory rate for my compositions. The controlled composition clause was designed for a physical era—in the streaming world, mechanicals are already reduced. Can we strike this provision?"

## 6. Audit Rights Restrictions

### What to Look For

**Problematic restrictions:**
- "Artist may audit no more than once every 3 years"
- "Audit must be conducted during regular business hours at Label's offices"
- "Artist bears all costs of audit unless discrepancy exceeds 10%"
- "Audit may only cover the most recent 2 years"
- "No audit after 24 months from statement date"

**Missing audit rights:**
- No mention of audit rights at all (rare but happens)

### Industry Standard

- Annual audit right
- Reasonable notice (30-60 days)
- If discrepancy >5%, label pays audit costs
- 3+ year lookback period

### Negotiation Tactics

**Script:**
> "I'd like standard audit rights: annual, with reasonable notice, and if there's a discrepancy of more than 5%, audit costs should be borne by the label. This is pretty standard and protects both parties."

## 7. Additional Red Flags to Watch

### Leaving Member Clause
- What happens if a band member leaves?
- Does the label retain rights to the departed member's future work?
- Can the label force the remaining members to replace the departed member?

### Key Man Clause (Missing)
- You signed because you trusted a specific A&R rep
- If they leave, you should have the right to exit or renegotiate
- Missing = you're stuck even if your champion is gone

### Territory
- "Universe" is a real term (anticipating space exploitation)
- Worldwide is standard
- Consider holding back specific territories if you have existing relationships

### Moral Rights Waiver
- Some contracts waive your "moral rights" (right to attribution, right to object to distortion)
- More common in work-for-hire situations
- Be aware of what you're giving up
