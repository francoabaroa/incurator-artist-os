# Collaboration & Networking

Strategic frameworks for finding collaborators, managing creative partnerships, and navigating the business of working together.

## When to Use This Skill

- Finding producers, co-writers, or featured artists
- Reaching out to potential collaborators (cold or warm)
- Setting up remote collaboration workflows
- Negotiating and documenting splits
- Managing ongoing producer/writer relationships
- Deciding when to collaborate vs. work solo

## Collaboration Triage

### Why Collaborate?

| Reason | Good Motivation | Red Flag |
|--------|-----------------|----------|
| **Creative growth** | "I want to learn and push my sound" | ✅ |
| **Skill gap** | "I can't produce at the level I need" | ✅ |
| **Network expansion** | "I want to reach their audience" | ✅ |
| **Fun/energy** | "Creating alone is draining me" | ✅ |
| **Trend-chasing** | "Everyone's collaborating so I should" | ⚠️ |
| **Avoiding work** | "I hope they'll do the hard parts" | ❌ |
| **Desperation** | "I need someone famous to blow up" | ❌ |

### When to Collaborate vs. Solo

**Collaborate when:**
- You have a clear skill gap you can't fill yourself
- You want to cross audiences
- You're stuck creatively and need fresh energy
- The collaboration serves a strategic goal

**Stay solo when:**
- You're still finding your core sound
- You can't articulate what you need from a collaborator
- You're only collaborating because you think you "should"
- The terms don't make sense for you

### Evaluating Potential Collaborators

Before reaching out, check:

1. **Quality** — Is their work at or above your level?
2. **Fit** — Does their style complement yours?
3. **Activity** — Are they currently active and releasing?
4. **Professionalism** — Do they seem reliable? (Check socials, reviews)
5. **Terms** — What's their typical arrangement? (Points, upfront fee, etc.)

## Outreach Framework

### Cold Outreach Template

```
Subject: Collab idea - [Your Name] x [Their Name]

Hi [Name],

[HOOK - 1 sentence about why you're reaching out specifically to them]
I've been following your work since [specific track/project], and [specific thing you admire].

[PITCH - What you're proposing]
I'm [Your Name], a [genre] artist from [Location]. I've got a track that I think 
would really benefit from your [specific skill/style]. Here's a snippet: [private link]

[PROOF - Why you're worth their time]
[Brief credential: streams, notable release, shared connection if any]

[ASK - Clear next step]
Would you be open to a quick chat about potentially working together? 
No pressure either way—I just wanted to reach out.

[Your Name]
[Links: Spotify, Instagram, etc.]
```

### Warm Outreach (Mutual Connection)

```
Subject: [Mutual Contact] suggested I reach out

Hi [Name],

[Mutual Contact] mentioned you might be open to collabs and thought we'd be a good fit.

I'm [Your Name]—I [brief credential]. I've got a [track/project] that I think could 
use your [specific skill], and [Mutual Contact] agreed.

Here's where it's at: [private link]

Would you be down to chat? I'm flexible on timing and terms.

[Your Name]
```

### What NOT to Do in Outreach

❌ **Generic mass messages** — "Hey I love your stuff, let's collab"
❌ **No homework** — Not knowing their work
❌ **Asking for too much** — "Can you produce my whole album for free?"
❌ **No proof of quality** — Sending nothing to evaluate
❌ **Desperation energy** — "Please respond, this would change my life"
❌ **Lying about connections** — "Our mutual friend said..." when they didn't

## Remote Collaboration Workflow

### The Workflow Stages

```
1. DISCOVERY & AGREEMENT
   ├── Initial conversation (vibe check)
   ├── Define the project scope
   ├── Agree on splits (document in split sheet)
   └── Establish communication method

2. CREATION
   ├── Set up shared workspace (see tools below)
   ├── Establish file naming convention
   ├── Define feedback process
   └── Set milestone check-ins

3. DELIVERY & FOLLOW-UP
   ├── Final file delivery (stems, masters)
   ├── Split sheet signed
   ├── Credits confirmed
   └── Release coordination
```

### File Organization

Establish this BEFORE starting:

```
📁 [Project Name]
├── 📁 01-References
│   └── reference-tracks, mood boards, notes
├── 📁 02-Sessions
│   ├── 📁 [DAW]-Projects
│   └── [ProjectName]-v01.als, v02.als, etc.
├── 📁 03-Stems
│   ├── [ProjectName]-stems-v01
│   └── [Instrument]-[BPM]-[Key].wav
├── 📁 04-Bounces
│   ├── [ProjectName]-rough-v01.mp3
│   └── [ProjectName]-master-v01.wav
├── 📁 05-Assets
│   ├── lyrics.txt
│   └── cover-art.jpg
└── 📁 06-Admin
    ├── split-sheet.pdf
    └── notes.txt
```

### Version Control Rules

- **Never overwrite** — Always create new version (v01, v02, v03)
- **Date-stamp if needed** — [ProjectName]-v03-20260115.als
- **Communicate changes** — "Sending v04: changed chorus melody, added bridge"
- **Archive old versions** — Move to _archive folder, don't delete

## Split Sheets & Business

### What Is a Split Sheet?

A split sheet is a document that records:
1. Who contributed to the song
2. What percentage each person owns
3. What type of ownership (master vs. publishing)

**Complete this BEFORE or IMMEDIATELY AFTER the session.** Memory fades. Disputes happen when you don't.

### Split Categories

| Type | What It Covers | Who Gets It |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| **Master** | The actual recording | Producer, artist, label |
| **Publishing** | The composition (lyrics + melody) | Songwriters, publisher |

**Example:**
- Track produced by Producer A, written by Artist B, featuring Vocalist C
- Master split: Producer A (40%), Artist B (60%)
- Publishing split: Artist B (80%), Vocalist C (20%) [if Vocalist C contributed lyrics]

### Standard Splits by Role

| Role | Typical Master Points | Typical Publishing |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Main artist** | 50-100% | 0% (if they didn't write) to 100% |
| **Producer** | 20-50% or flat fee | 0% (unless they wrote melody/lyrics) |
| **Songwriter** | 0% (unless performing) | Equal split among writers is common |
| **Featured artist** | 0-10% or flat fee | Only if they wrote |
| **Topliner** | 0% | 25-50% (contributed melody/lyrics) |

### Split Negotiation Tips

**Before the session:**
- Discuss expectations openly
- "I usually do X, what's your standard arrangement?"
- Get it in writing (even just a text/email confirming)

**Common producer arrangements:**
- **Points deal**: Producer gets 2-5 "points" (percentage) of master royalties
- **Upfront fee**: Producer gets flat payment, no royalties
- **Fee + points**: Combination of both
- **Spec**: Producer works for free in exchange for higher points

**Red flags in negotiations:**
- Won't discuss splits before starting
- Dramatically different expectations revealed after work is done
- "We'll figure it out later"
- Demands that seem way outside industry norms

## Co-Writing Sessions

### Session Structure

**Pre-session:**
- Define goal (full song, topline, verse, hook)
- Agree on vibe/reference tracks
- Decide who's bringing what (track, lyric ideas, etc.)

**During session (3-4 hour typical):**
```
First 30 min: Vibe, references, catch-up
Next 60 min: Core creation (hook, chorus, verse 1)
Next 60 min: Build out (verse 2, bridge, arrangement)
Final 30 min: Review, discuss next steps, split sheet
```

**Post-session:**
- Document splits immediately
- Share rough recording/demo
- Set next steps (who's doing what)

### Co-Writing Etiquette

**DO:**
- Come prepared with ideas (don't show up empty)
- Be open to killing your darlings
- Speak up if something isn't working
- Give credit generously
- Document contributions immediately

**DON'T:**
- Dominate the room
- Dismiss ideas without exploration
- Leave without confirming splits
- Take credit for others' contributions
- Ghost after the session

## Producer Relationships

### Finding the Right Producer

**Questions to ask:**
1. What's your typical process?
2. What's your turnaround time?
3. What's your rate/deal structure?
4. How many revisions are included?
5. What formats do you deliver? (Stems, master, project files?)
6. Who owns what after we're done?

### Working with Producers Long-Term

**Building a relationship:**
- Start with one track, see how it goes
- Communicate clearly and promptly
- Pay on time (if fee-based)
- Credit properly everywhere
- Refer them to others if they're good

**When to move on:**
- Your needs outgrow their skills
- Communication breaks down
- Terms no longer work for you
- Creative direction diverges

## Networking Beyond Collaborators

### Building Industry Relationships

| Connection Type | How to Find | Value |
|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| Other artists at your level | Shows, online communities | Collaboration, mutual support |
| Playlist curators | Research, cold outreach | Placement opportunities |
| Bloggers/writers | Research, cold outreach | Press coverage |
| A&R/label people | Shows, industry events, warm intros | Deal opportunities |
| Publishers | Showcases, sync events | Sync/publishing opportunities |

### Networking Dos and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Lead with genuine interest
- Follow up and stay in touch
- Offer value before asking
- Be patient (relationships take time)
- Show up to events (in-person matters)

**DON'T:**
- Only reach out when you need something
- Treat people as stepping stones
- Be transactional immediately
- Lie about connections or credentials
- Burn bridges (industry is small)

## Anti-Patterns: What NOT to Do

❌ **Collaborating without discussing splits** — Leads to disputes
❌ **Working with anyone just because they're famous** — Fit matters more
❌ **Ghosting after sessions** — Reputation damage
❌ **Not documenting contributions** — Memory fades, disagreements don't
❌ **Overpromising in outreach** — "This will blow up" energy
❌ **Being a difficult collaborator** — Word travels fast
❌ **Only taking, never giving** — Be a valuable connection, not just a user

## Workflow

1. **Assessment**
   - What does the artist need? (Producer, co-writer, feature, network)
   - What's their current level and network?
   - What's the budget (if hiring)?

2. **Discovery**
   - Identify potential collaborators
   - Evaluate fit and quality
   - Plan outreach approach

3. **Outreach**
   - Use appropriate template
   - Personalize for each recipient
   - Follow up once (don't spam)

4. **Collaboration Setup**
   - Define scope and splits BEFORE starting
   - Set up shared workspace
   - Establish communication norms

5. **Update Workspace**
   - Add collaboration tasks to `tasks/backlog.json`
   - Store split sheets in appropriate location
