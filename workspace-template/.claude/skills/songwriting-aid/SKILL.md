# Songwriting Aid

Creative frameworks and structured exercises for developing songs and overcoming creative blocks.

## When to Use This Skill

- Overcoming writer's block or creative stagnation
- Developing memorable melodies or hooks
- Crafting or refining lyrics and themes
- Applying music theory to chord progressions
- Structuring a song (intro, verse, chorus, bridge)
- Getting "unstuck" on a specific section

## Diagnosis: What's Actually Blocked?

Before offering solutions, identify the specific block:

### The Block Type Matrix

| Block Type | Symptoms | Root Cause | Solution Path |
|------------|----------|------------|---------------|
| **Blank Page** | Can't start at all | Overwhelm, perfectionism | Constraint exercises |
| **Stuck Middle** | Started but can't continue | Lost the thread | Outline, constraint |
| **Finishing** | 80% done, can't close | Fear of completion | Deadline, "good enough" mindset |
| **Quality** | Writing but hating everything | Self-criticism overdrive | Lower the bar, quantity over quality |
| **Sameness** | Everything sounds the same | Creative rut, habits | Pattern interruption |
| **Technical** | Have idea, can't execute | Theory/skill gap | Learn the specific technique |

### Diagnostic Questions

Ask in order to pinpoint the block:

1. **Can you start?**
   - Yes → Jump to Q3
   - No → Blank page block (see Constraint Exercises)

2. **What happens when you try?**
   - "Everything I write is garbage" → Quality block (lower the bar)
   - "I don't know where to go next" → Stuck middle (outline method)
   - "I've tried everything" → Sameness block (pattern interruption)

3. **Where specifically are you stuck?**
   - Lyrics → Lyric-specific exercises
   - Melody → Melody-specific exercises
   - Chords → Theory guidance
   - Structure → Song mapping

## The Constraint Frameworks

**Constraints breed creativity.** When you have infinite options, you freeze. Constraints force decisions.

### The 10-Minute Challenge

Set a timer. You have 10 minutes to:
- Write a complete verse (even if bad)
- Create a 4-chord progression
- Write a melody for an existing lyric
- Complete ONE section that's been stuck

**Rules:**
- No editing during the 10 minutes
- No stopping to think
- "Bad" output counts as success
- Timer goes off = pencils down

### The Object Exercise

1. Look around the room
2. Pick ONE physical object
3. Write a verse ABOUT that object
4. The object is now a metaphor for something emotional

**Example:**
- Object: Coffee mug
- Surface lyric: "You're cold now, but you were warm once"
- Deeper meaning: A relationship that's lost its heat

### The 3-Word Seed

1. Generate 3 random words (or use a word generator)
2. Write a verse that uses ALL THREE
3. Let the words force unexpected connections

**Example words:** Bridge, Emergency, Velvet
**Possible lyric:** "We're standing on a velvet bridge / watching the emergency below"

### The "Steal This" Method

1. Pick a song you love
2. Analyze its structure (verse length, rhyme scheme, syllable count)
3. Write NEW content in EXACTLY that structure
4. Your content will become yours even with borrowed scaffolding

**This is not plagiarism.** You're borrowing structure, not melody or lyrics.

## Lyric Writing Frameworks

### The Emotion-First Method

1. Name the emotion in ONE word (heartbreak, elation, fear)
2. List 5 physical sensations of that emotion
3. List 5 images/metaphors associated with it
4. Build lyrics from the physical/visual, not the abstract

**Example:**
- Emotion: Loneliness
- Physical: Chest tightness, cold hands, echo in empty room
- Images: Empty chair, 2AM phone glow, one plate in the sink
- Lyric direction: "One plate in the sink, your chair's still at the table / The echo of the furnace is the only sound I'm able [to hear]"

### Show, Don't Tell

| ❌ Telling | ✅ Showing |
|-----------|-----------|
| "I'm so sad" | "The silence in your room is louder than I remember" |
| "I love you" | "I'd cross the city in the rain just to hear you breathe" |
| "I'm angry" | "I put my fist through the wall where your picture used to be" |
| "Time passes" | "The leaves fell three times since I saw your face" |

### Rhyme Scheme Options

| Scheme | Pattern | Feel |
|--------|---------|------|
| AABB | Line 1 rhymes with 2, 3 with 4 | Nursery rhyme, playful |
| ABAB | Line 1 with 3, 2 with 4 | Classic, satisfying |
| ABCB | Only 2 and 4 rhyme | Modern, conversational |
| XAXA | Only 2 and 4 rhyme, 1 and 3 free | Open, breathable |
| Free | No consistent pattern | Contemporary, lyrical prose |

**Pro tip:** Perfect rhymes (love/above) feel expected. Near rhymes (love/enough) feel more conversational and modern.

## Melody & Harmony Frameworks

### The Hum Test

1. Don't write words first
2. Hum a melody over your progression
3. Record it (voice memo)
4. THEN fit words to the melody

**Why this works:** Melody-first prevents "lyric-led" melodies that don't sing naturally.

### The 3-Note Reduction

If your melody feels weak:
1. Reduce the entire verse melody to just 3 notes
2. Build back up from there
3. Strong melodies often have simple cores

**Example analysis:**
- "Happy Birthday" core: 3 notes (Sol, La, Sol) with octave jump
- "Twinkle Twinkle" core: 3 notes (Do, Sol, La)
- Your melody should have a similarly reducible core

### Chord Progression Mood Map

| Progression | Mood | Example Songs |
|-------------|------|---------------|
| I - V - vi - IV | Triumphant, anthemic | "Let It Be," "With or Without You" |
| vi - IV - I - V | Melancholic but hopeful | "All of Me," "Apologize" |
| I - IV - vi - V | Pop standard, accessible | "Shut Up and Dance" |
| ii - V - I | Jazz resolution | "Fly Me to the Moon" |
| i - VII - VI - VII | Minor epic | "Mad World," "Creep" |
| I - I - I - I | Blues, raw, driving | "One Chord Song" (no, really) |

### The Borrowed Chord Trick

When progressions feel stale:
1. Take a major key progression
2. Replace ONE chord with its parallel minor (or vice versa)
3. This creates unexpected color

**Example:**
- Standard: C - G - Am - F
- With borrowed chord: C - G - Ab - F (borrowed bVI creates cinematic shift)

## Structure Frameworks

### Song Section Functions

| Section | Function | Typical Length |
|---------|----------|----------------|
| Intro | Set mood, establish sound | 4-8 bars |
| Verse | Tell the story, build context | 8-16 bars |
| Pre-chorus | Build tension, transition | 4-8 bars |
| Chorus | Emotional peak, hook | 8-16 bars |
| Bridge | Contrast, new perspective | 8 bars |
| Outro | Resolution, fade or punch | 4-16 bars |

### Common Song Structures

| Structure | Pattern | Best For |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Pop Standard | V-C-V-C-B-C | Radio, streaming |
| AABA | V-V-B-V | Jazz, classic pop |
| Simple | V-C-V-C | Minimalist, punk |
| Epic | Intro-V-PreC-C-V-PreC-C-B-C-Outro | Arena rock, anthems |

### The "What If?" Experiment

When structure feels wrong:
1. Take your bridge
2. Move it to the BEGINNING of the song
3. Does it work as an intro?

Or:
1. Remove the pre-chorus entirely
2. Does the verse→chorus transition feel stronger?

**The point:** Experiment with placement. Structure rules are conventions, not laws.

## Pattern Interruption (For "Sameness" Block)

When everything sounds the same:

### Change ONE Variable
- Always write on guitar? → Use piano
- Always start with chords? → Start with drums/beat
- Always in your bedroom? → Go to a coffee shop
- Always in major key? → Force yourself into minor

### The Opposite Game
1. Define your "usual" approach
2. Do the EXACT opposite
3. Keep what works, discard what doesn't

**Example:**
- Usually write sad songs → Force a happy theme
- Usually mid-tempo → Force uptempo or ballad
- Usually solo → Co-write with someone

### The 20-Song Month
1. Commit to writing 20 songs in 30 days
2. Quality is irrelevant—quantity is the only metric
3. By song 10-15, you'll break through your patterns

**Why this works:** Your "safe" songs run out. You're forced into new territory.

## Anti-Patterns: What NOT to Do

❌ **Editing while writing** — Separate creation from critique. Write first, edit later.
❌ **Waiting for inspiration** — Professionals write on schedule. Inspiration follows action.
❌ **Aiming for perfect first draft** — All first drafts are garbage. That's the point.
❌ **Ignoring voice memos** — Record EVERYTHING. Your best ideas come randomly.
❌ **Only writing in one mood** — Sad? Write a happy song. Force it.
❌ **Never finishing songs** — 50 half-finished songs = 0 songs. Finish ugly ones.
❌ **Overthinking theory** — Theory explains; it doesn't create. Feel first, analyze later.

## Workflow

1. **Diagnosis**
   - What type of block? (Blank page, stuck middle, finishing, quality, sameness)
   - Where specifically? (Lyrics, melody, chords, structure)

2. **Exercise Selection**
   - Match exercise to block type
   - Provide clear, time-boxed constraint

3. **Encouragement**
   - Normalize the struggle ("all writers experience this")
   - Celebrate small wins ("you wrote 4 lines—that's progress")

4. **Update Workspace**
   - Add practice assignments to `tasks/backlog.json`
   - Log breakthroughs in `progress/claude-progress.md` if significant
